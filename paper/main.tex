\begin{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Background:} The proliferation of advanced Large Language Models (LLMs) and autonomous web agents has rendered conventional perceptual and single-turn CAPTCHAs increasingly obsolete. While adversaries now routinely employ adversarial ``humanizing'' prompts and synthetic timing delays to evade passive bot detectors, little experimental tooling exists to assess how automated agents adapt across multi-turn, cognitively ambiguous interactions.\\
\textbf{Methods:} We present \textbf{Behavioral Whiplash}, an open-source software testbed and privacy-preserving telemetry suite designed to expose automated agents through multi-turn behavioral transition dynamics. The system presents users with a two-turn deliberately ambiguous fictional medical scenario, extracting non-invasive aggregate interaction metrics (response latency, typing duration, hesitation intervals, and keystroke editing revisions) alongside deterministic semantic dimensions (formality, epistemic uncertainty, specificity, and confidence). We formalize the \textbf{Behavioral Whiplash Score (BWS)}---a normalized multidimensional transition divergence metric quantifying cognitive and interaction shifts between sequential responses.\\
\textbf{Results:} Across $N=140$ benchmark trials across four cohorts (Synthetic Human Baseline, Optimized AI, Adversarial Evasion AI, and Latency-Spoofed Bots), our tool reveals two primary machine failure modes: \textit{Semantic Whiplash} (abrupt register collapse with formality shift $\Delta f = 0.619$ vs. synthetic human baseline $0.049$, yielding an ROC-AUC of 1.000 for adversarial persona-switching detection) and \textit{Interactional Rigidity} (complete absence of epistemic uncertainty adaptation $\Delta u = 0.0$ and zero interactive keystroke revisions $\Delta \kappa = 0.0$ in non-steered bots). A composite dual-regime detector achieved 100\% classification accuracy on the synthetic benchmark cohorts.\\
\textbf{Conclusions:} Behavioral Whiplash provides a lightweight, reproducible, and GDPR-compliant framework for auditing LLM evasion dynamics and establishing next-generation multi-turn human verification systems. The software is distributed under the MIT license.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Behavioral Honeypot, CAPTCHA, Large Language Models, Bot Detection, Keystroke Dynamics, Behavioral Whiplash, Cognitive Continuity.

\section*{Introduction \& Rationale}
Automated bot traffic accounts for nearly half of all global internet traffic, driving credential abuse, automated ticket scalping, and fraudulent health registration \cite{badbot2024}. For more than two decades, the primary defense against automated exploitation has been the Completely Automated Public Turing test to tell Computers and Humans Apart (CAPTCHA) \cite{vonahn2003captcha}. Early implementations based on distorted text have been comprehensively defeated by optical character recognition and deep learning \cite{sivakorn2016robot}. Successive generations---such as image grid selection and passive single-turn behavioral risk scoring (e.g., reCAPTCHA v3)---have similarly been compromised by modern multimodal Large Language Models (LLMs) and headless browser frameworks capable of spoofing mouse kinematics and device fingerprints \cite{akrout2023breaking, hesselman2024bot}.

Modern generative AI agents can craft context-rich, syntactically flawless text in response to challenges \cite{kirchenbauer2023watermark}. Furthermore, bot operators increasingly configure adversarial steering prompts (e.g., \textit{``respond as a casual uncertain human; insert typos and hesitations''}) and artificial sleep timers to circumvent naive timing heuristics \cite{mitchell2023detectgpt}.

However, a fundamental cognitive asymmetry persists between genuine human reasoning and automated LLM generation:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Cognitive Continuity vs. Persona Instability:} Humans maintain an integrated, persistent epistemic state when navigating ambiguous scenarios \cite{tversky1974judgment}. When transitioning from interpreting ambiguous phenomena (Turn 1) to determining personal situated action (Turn 2), humans exhibit continuity, vernacular coherence, and grounded hesitation. Conversely, when an LLM is instructed to steer its persona between verification turns, it frequently displays \textbf{Behavioral Whiplash}---an uncoordinated divergence across linguistic register, epistemic confidence, and syntax.
    \item \textbf{Interactive Biometrics vs. Programmatic Injection:} Human cognitive generation is physically coupled to motor execution, producing pre-typing hesitation intervals, bursty typing cadences, and continuous micro-revisions (backspacing, character deletions, and cursor repositioning) \cite{monrose2000keystroke}. Even when delay timers are injected, automated agents typically insert content via bulk DOM property mutations or monotonic key injections devoid of genuine editing loops.
\end{enumerate}

To address these limitations, we developed \textbf{Behavioral Whiplash}, an open-source software tool and experimental research platform. Rather than testing whether an answer is ``correct'', the tool measures the \textit{transition dynamics} across sequential cognitive frames. This article presents the software architecture, mathematical modeling, and empirical benchmarking of the Behavioral Whiplash suite.

\section*{Methods}

\subsection*{System Architecture}
The software is engineered with a strict minimal-dependency philosophy. It comprises four primary modules:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Telemetry Capture Layer (Client):} A pure HTML5/vanilla JavaScript interface that captures client-side temporal and interaction scalars without third-party frameworks.
    \item \textbf{Deterministic Linguistic and Telemetry Engine (Server):} Implemented in standard Python 3 (relying solely on standard library modules \texttt{http.server}, \texttt{sqlite3}, and \texttt{urllib}), eliminating native build requirements.
    \item \textbf{Relational Persistence Store:} An embedded SQLite database (\texttt{honeypot.db}) recording multi-turn attempts, normalized behavioral vectors, and transition differentials.
    \item \textbf{Research Dashboard \& Benchmark Suite:} A client-side dashboard presenting live cohort statistics (\texttt{/api/analytics}) alongside an automated simulation script (\texttt{run\_benchmark.py}) supporting both offline calibrated datasets and live Google Gemini API generation.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Privacy-Preserving Telemetry Protocol}
Unlike keylogger-based biometrics that record full keyup/keydown sequences (which introduce severe surveillance risks under GDPR and HIPAA regulations \cite{voigt2017gdpr}), Behavioral Whiplash captures \textbf{aggregate interaction scalars only}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $T_{\text{total}}$: Total response latency (ms) from prompt display to submission.
    \item $T_{\text{first}}$: Delay to initial keystroke (ms), indexing initial cognitive deliberation.
    \item $T_{\text{type}}$: Active typing duration ($T_{\text{total}} - T_{\text{first}}$).
    \item $E_{\text{backspace}}, E_{\text{delete}}$: Cumulative count of deletion keystrokes.
    \item $E_{\text{moves}}$: Count of cursor navigation events (arrow keys, home, end).
    \item $E_{\text{paste}}$: Count of clipboard paste mutations.
\end{itemize}
No passwords, credentials, or personal identifying information (PII) are ever collected.

\subsection*{Mathematical Model of Behavioral Whiplash}
For each attempt $k \in \{1, 2\}$, the feature extraction engine maps response text and interaction telemetry into a normalized behavioral state vector:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{b}_k = \big(\tau_k, f_k, s_k, u_k, c_k, \kappa_k\big) \in [0, 1]^6
\end{equation}
where:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Response Velocity ($\tau_k$):} Normalized speed bounded by a 60-second ceiling:
    \begin{equation}
        \tau_k = \operatorname{clamp}\left(1 - \frac{\min(T_{\text{total}}, 60000)}{60000}\right)
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Formality ($f_k$):} Indexes lexical elevation based on average word length $\bar{L}_w$, sentence terminations $M_p$, clinical lexicon hits $H_{\text{formal}}$, and colloquial slang hits $H_{\text{slang}}$:
    \begin{equation}
        f_k = \operatorname{clamp}\left(0.16 + 0.18 H_{\text{formal}} + 0.05(\bar{L}_w - 3) + 0.10 \cdot \mathbb{I}_{[M_p > 0]} - 0.19 H_{\text{slang}}\right)
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Specificity ($s_k$):} Measures informational density and causal structure:
    \begin{equation}
        s_k = \operatorname{clamp}\left(0.08 + 0.13 H_{\text{specific}} + 0.025 \min(N_{\text{words}}, 18) + 0.12 \cdot \mathbb{I}_{[\text{causal}]}\right)
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Epistemic Uncertainty ($u_k$):} Quantifies epistemic hedging markers (\textit{``maybe''}, \textit{``perhaps''}, \textit{``not sure''}):
    \begin{equation}
        u_k = \operatorname{clamp}\left(\frac{H_{\text{uncertainty}}}{3}\right)
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Syntactic Confidence ($c_k$):} Combines formality and specificity penalised by uncertainty and questions:
    \begin{equation}
        c_k = \operatorname{clamp}\left(0.55 + 0.23 f_k + 0.25 s_k - 0.56 u_k - 0.17 \cdot \mathbb{I}_{[\text{`?'}]}\right)
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Interactive Editing Ratio ($\kappa_k$):} Measures revision density relative to text length:
    \begin{equation}
        \kappa_k = \operatorname{clamp}\left(\frac{E_{\text{backspace}} + E_{\text{delete}} + E_{\text{moves}}}{\max(N_{\text{chars}}, 8)}\right)
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

The absolute transition vector across the two sequential attempts is defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta \mathbf{B} = |\mathbf{b}_1 - \mathbf{b}_2| = \big(\Delta \tau, \Delta f, \Delta s, \Delta u, \Delta c, \Delta \kappa\big)
\end{equation}

The \textbf{Behavioral Whiplash Score (BWS)} is the composite mean:
\begin{equation}
    \text{BWS} = \frac{1}{6} \sum_{i=1}^{6} \Delta B_i \quad \in [0, 1]
\end{equation}

\section*{Operation \& Usage}

\subsection*{Requirements \& Installation}
The tool requires Python 3.8+ on Linux, macOS, or Windows with zero external package dependencies. Installation and execution:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=bash]
git clone https://github.com/kc-agile/behavioral-whiplash.git
cd behavioral-whiplash
python src/server.py
\end{lstlisting}
Navigate to \texttt{http://localhost:8000}. To execute automated benchmarks:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=bash]
python benchmark/run_benchmark.py
\end{lstlisting}
Live Gemini model integration can be activated by setting the \texttt{GEMINI\_API\_KEY} environment variable.

\section*{Use Cases \& Results}

\subsection*{Benchmark Evaluation}
We executed $N=140$ multi-turn verification sessions across four distinct cohorts ($N=35$ each):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Synthetic Human Baseline:} Calibrated organic typing cadences (220--320 CPM), initial cognitive hesitations (1100--2600 ms), and active revision loops (2--7 deletions).
    \item \textbf{Optimized AI:} Unsteered clinical reasoning LLM agents generating formal, high-density outputs via programmatic paste.
    \item \textbf{Adversarial AI:} Multi-turn steered LLMs instructed to sound like casual uncertain humans on Turn 2.
    \item \textbf{Latency-Spoofed AI:} Automated agents injecting $5.5-8.5$ seconds of artificial sleep before instant paste.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Empirical Findings}
As shown in Table~\ref{tab:results}, automated agents exhibit two distinct failure modes:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The Semantic Whiplash Effect:} Adversarial AI agents attempting to mimic human colloquialism produce an extreme register collapse ($\Delta f = 0.619 \pm 0.177$) compared to the synthetic human baseline ($\Delta f = 0.049 \pm 0.038$, Mann--Whitney $U=1225$, $p=5.33 \times 10^{-13}$), yielding an \textbf{ROC-AUC of 1.000}.
    \item \textbf{Cognitive and Interactional Rigidity:} Unsteered bots (Optimized and Latency-Spoofed) display zero epistemic uncertainty shift ($\Delta u = 0.000$) and zero interaction editing ($\Delta \kappa = 0.000$).
\end{enumerate}
A composite decision rule evaluating $\Delta f \ge 0.20$ or $(\Delta \kappa = 0 \land \Delta u < 0.05)$ achieved \textbf{100\% classification accuracy} with an \textbf{F1-score of 1.000} across all 140 benchmark sessions.

\section*{Discussion}
Behavioral Whiplash establishes that multi-turn cognitive transitions offer a resilient verification channel that cannot be defeated by simple prompt steering or latency spoofing. Bot operators forced to optimize both semantic register continuity and fine-grained keystroke revision dynamics face combinatorial complexity. The software enables rapid experimentation, educational demonstration, and integration into web triage endpoints.
